1 · Overview

A homegrown discipline, a deliberate climb

Development Communication is a Filipino discipline. Its academic lineage goes back to the 1960s at the University of the Philippines Los Baños, inside the Department of Agricultural Information and Communication (UPCA-DAIC), where it began as agricultural journalism and grew into a global social science. DEVC 202 is the foundational gateway of the Master of Development Communication program: it was first built as the introductory component of the MPS program, and it has evolved with the field ever since.

The course is designed as a climb. We begin with society's visible problems, move through the communication process and its media, and end at the theory and policy frames of DevCom practice.

What I should be able to do by the end

  • Explain the underdevelopment problematique.
  • Cite and compare basic models, theories, and concepts of communication.
  • Discuss the function and role of communication in the development process.
  • Define development communication.
  • Articulate a philosophy of development communication.
  • Describe the roles and competencies of a development communication practitioner.
  • Illustrate and display understanding through an original knowledge product.

2 · Unit I · Development & the underdevelopment problematique

Eight problems that keep coming back

DevCom emerged as a response to eight virulent clusters of problems in post-colonial, developing nations. The statistics below are the ones the course keeps repeating, on purpose: the point is that we must not grow numb to them.

ProblemThe course numbers
PovertyThe root menace. Some 35.2 million Filipinos live in absolute poverty, unable to meet basic needs for food, shelter, or health.
Unemployment & underemploymentLack of livelihood, and the waste of human resources: professionals such as dentists and teachers taking menial overseas jobs to survive.
High population growthSeven of the world's ten most populous nations are developing countries, straining limited resources.
InequalityIn the Philippines, 10% of the population controls 90% of economic resources. The 1998 UNDP report: the 3 richest individuals held more wealth than the total GDP of 48 nations; the 15 richest more than Sub-Saharan Africa; the 32 richest more than South Asia.
Environmental degradationThe Philippines lost about 70% of its primary forests in 50 years; arable land keeps converting to industrial zones, threatening food security.
MalnutritionUNICEF estimate: 160,000 children die annually from malnutrition. It wears two faces: undernutrition in poor nations, overnutrition (obesity among the poor) in industrialized ones.
Ethnic conflictModern clashes of cultures rather than purely political wars: Mindanao, Rwanda, Bosnia.
Societal prioritiesGlobal spending skews to military hardware and even pet food, while basic education and sanitation receive a fraction of those funds.

The problematique method (Molenda & Di Paolo)

A problematique is a cluster of interrelated, virulent problems that recur if not addressed at the source.

  • Subordinate influential factors are the symptoms: specific illnesses, temporary hunger. Treating them offers only a palliative, a temporary fix.
  • Superordinate influential factors are the root causes, the underlying drivers. Sustainable development means identifying and eradicating these.

What development is: the hatching egg and the moth

  • Internal force (self-activity). Real development must come from within. Break an eggshell from the outside and you get an aborted life, not a chick.
  • External facilitation (the incubator). Science and development workers provide the conditions: irrigation, credit, heat. But the community itself must do the struggle of breaking the shell, or it will not be strong enough to survive.
  • Seers' conditions. Development is happening only when poverty, unemployment, and inequality all decrease at the same time.

Four paradigms of underdevelopment

ParadigmWhere it locates the problem
TechnologicalA lack of modern tools and techniques.
EconomicCapital, productivity, and investment returns.
StructuralPolitical systems and the distribution of power.
ValuesThe internal attitudes and cultural beliefs of the society.
3 · Unit II · Communication process & media

From transmission to process

The course pushes a paradigm shift: communication is not a one-way transmission but a process. Kincaid and Schramm define it as "the process of sharing and the relationship of the participants in the process."

The act of communication: Lasswell's five questions

QuestionResearch subdivision
Who?Control analysis
Says what?Content analysis
In which channel?Media analysis
To whom?Audience analysis
With what effect?Effect analysis

Interpersonal vs mass media

CharacteristicInterpersonal mediaMass media
Senses stimulated3 or more, often all1 or 2 at a time
Opportunity for feedbackHigh, immediateLow, delayed
Control of paceReceiver and senderSender
Multiplicative powerLowHigh
Direction of flowTwo-wayPrimarily one-way
Message accuracyLower, subject to changeHigher
Ability to meet specialized needsHighLow
Speed to reach a large audienceSlowFast

Barriers to effectiveness: the noise

  • Physical noise. Environmental sound, plus technological noise: alerts, notifications, overflowing inboxes.
  • Psychological-cultural noise. Stereotypes, negative attitudes, regionalism.
  • Social noise. Disparities in social class or status that distort how a message is received.
4 · Unit III · DevCom foundations & practice

The definition to know cold

The art and science of human communication applied to the speedy transformation of a country from poverty to economic growth and social equality.
- Nora C. Quebral, 1971

The modern version: the science of human communication linked to the transitioning of communities from poverty in all its forms to growth that fosters equity and individual potential. DevCom is a science because it uses systematic research and social science methods; it is an art because it takes the creative hand of writers and artists to make information meaningful.

Lasswell's three functions of communication

  • Surveillance - scanning the environment for threats and opportunities.
  • Correlation - organizing society's response to the environment.
  • Transmission - passing social heritage, values and skills, to the next generation.

The democratic goal attached to these is equivalent enlightenment: the layman's understanding of reality matching the expert's general estimate.

Five areas of practice

  • Communication policy and planning
  • Development journalism
  • Educational communication
  • Community media
  • Social marketing

As a policy science, DevCom involves stakeholders - government, media, consumer groups - in creating communication plans that shape the national environment.

The proactive information society (MUAR model scenario, A. Flor)

Reactive role Follow market supply and demand. Maintain the status quo.
Proactive role Shape the environment. Work toward a positive, equitable future. The choice the course argues for.

6 · The outputs

What each requirement looks like

TMA 1: Societal Problems scrapbook (due 27 September)

Read Chapters 1-4 first, then begin a scrapbook of the eight major societal problems, organized into headings matching those problems. It may be made of any material: a blank record book, bound scratch paper, a three-ring binder, or an electronic format. Collect webpages, newspaper articles, digital photographs, magazine clippings, posters, or leaflets; items should be no more than one year old. A table of contents is required. There is an official scrapbook reference in the course pack. Required inventory table:

Item no.Item descriptionDate
1e.g. Newspaper clipping: malnutrition rates
2e.g. Digital image: poverty incidence

TMA 2: Poverty study (due 25 October)

Read Chapters 5-8. An in-depth study on why Filipinos remain poor despite improvements in daily life, viewed through any dimension: economic, social, political, environmental, cultural, or mental; urban or rural. The steps:

  • Choose your respondents: 20 if quantitative, or participants if qualitative.
  • Choose a communication theory that can help explain the state of being poor.
  • Gather data: online survey questionnaire (quantitative) or key informant interview guide (qualitative).
  • Tabulate the data or encode the transcripts.
  • Using five journal references from the last five years, discuss the results in narrative form: insights and implications.
  • Write the report following the sequence of your objectives, with a socio-demographic table of respondents: age, sex, educational attainment, income level, electronic gadgets used, internet connectivity, and other relevant information.
  • Formulate recommendations based on your findings.

TMA 3: Proactive society critique (due 22 November)

Read Chapters 9-13. An analysis or critique of the scenario and opinions presented over the past 30 years, based on Dr. Flor's "The Proactive Information Society". The Course Guide says five pages or less: succinct and direct to the point, supported by what the textbook teaches. Each unit in the course's table of contents corresponds to one TMA.

Reflection Synthesis Piece (due 6 December)

A creative knowledge product reflecting my personal interpretation of DevCom: a song, poem, TikTok, short story, or similar original piece.

The vlog: Looking Back, Moving Forward (due 18 December)

  • A 5-minute video reflection, also serving as the oral assessment.
  • Show the journal on camera.
  • Answer ONE of the five prompts: (1) Learning, (2) Application, (3) Reflection, (4) Connection, or (5) Personal Insight.
  • File naming protocol: Surname_FirstName_DEVC202_OralAssessment - mine will be Noda_Arnold_DEVC202_OralAssessment.
