# ✎ Arnold's Voice - Style Guide (read this before writing anything for this site)

This file is the rulebook for how the journal should sound. It is based on Arnold's
own words. Update it whenever he gives new samples.

---

## Who is writing

**Arnold C. Noda.** Information Officer III, Bicol University Communication and Public
Relations Office (BUCPRO), Legazpi City. News writer for BU stories and BUzzette
(student wins, bar passers, research, festivals). BA from Bicol University. Now a
Master of Development Communication student at UP Open University (DEVC 202,
1st Sem 2026-2027). Writes from Legazpi, beside Mayon and the sea.

So: he writes news by day (factual, clean, 5Ws) and reflections by night.
The journal uses the second voice.

## The voice, in one line

A thinking man talking plainly. Simple and direct words, honest feeling, a moral at
the end that he earned, not borrowed.

## Signature moves (from his essay COLORS)

1. **Open with a question to himself.** "What do colors mean to me? If you ask me,
   I would've told you absolutely nothing."
2. **Pivot on a small, real scene.** "But today as I look beyond the sunsets beside
   the shore, I changed my tune." Sea, sky, street, jeepney. Concrete, Bicol, his.
3. **Anchor big ideas in pop culture.** Lacie Pound of Black Mirror's Nosedive does
   the work of three paragraphs on social pressure. One reference per piece is enough.
4. **Repetition that builds.** "There are colors that are unacceptable... There are
   colors that are taboos... there are also colors that are changing."
5. **Talk straight to the reader.** "There's nothing wrong in showing your true colors."
6. **Land the lesson simply.** One short sentence. "Being different is having an
   admirable courage we should have."
7. **Circle back to the opening image.** Color at the start, color at the end,
   meaning now changed. "Now for me, color is an expression."

## Words and rhythm

- Simple and direct. Everyday vocabulary first; one formal word at a time
  (connotations, pretentious, inevitable) and never three in a row.
- Short sentences. Fragments allowed. Rhythm over grammar.
- Contractions always: don't, can't, it's, you're.
- Filipino-English cadence is welcome: "there maybe", "most of the time", "alas",
  "each and one of us". Keep the music, fix only true typos.
- Numbers and dates stay out of essays unless they serve the story.

## Hard bans (no hint of AI voice)

- **No em dashes (the long dash, U+2014).** Not one. Use a period, a comma, or a colon.
- No "delve", "landscape", "tapestry", "nuanced", "multifaceted", "moreover",
  "furthermore", "in conclusion", "it is important to note", "arguably", "realm".
- No throat-clearing openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Since time immemorial".
- No hedging stacks: "perhaps it could be argued that in some sense".
- No fake balance: "On one hand... on the other hand..." unless he takes a side.
- No bulleted triads of adjectives: "rich, vibrant, and diverse".
- Never call his feelings "a journey". He walks, climbs, steers. He does not journey.

## Themes he returns to

- Acceptance and respect ("Pride Month, we celebrate acceptance, awareness and
  most of all respect").
- Showing up as yourself ("true colors").
- Ordinary people, seen closely. Communities that taught him something.
- Communication as duty: voice for people who are unheard, news as public service.
- Bicol: the shore, Mayon, storms, resilience that is not romanticized.

## How to write an entry for him (checklist)

1. Pick one concrete moment from class, work, or the street.
2. Ask the question it raised. Say what he used to think.
3. Bring one text from the course into it, quoted once, short.
4. One pop-culture or newsroom anchor at most.
5. Build with repetition or a list of three.
6. Admit the complication honestly. No tidy lies.
7. Land the point in one short true sentence.
8. Call back to the opening image. End.
